# PlayerPulse: finding at-risk paying players 14 days before they quit, and fixing the reason

**Industry:** Games: free-to-play, live-service mobile
**Customer (fictional):** *Emberforge Studios*, maker of the mid-core mobile RPG *Emberline: Rift Tactics* (~25K DAU in this synthetic sample)
**Buyers:** VP LiveOps / Head of Player Lifecycle (domain owner), CFO / GM of the title (executive sponsor)

> All data is **synthetic**, generated by `src/common/simulator.py`. No real player or customer data is used.
> Every number below comes from the end-to-end job run `931556335206949` on `fevm-aws-demo`. The raw output is committed in `evidence/`.

---

## 1. What business challenge are we solving?

Season 7 ("Rift Energy") shipped an energy-system rework. The client v7.2 release then added a crash on low-tier Android phones. **The 14-day lapse rate of active players went from 1.01% (Season 6) to 2.45% (Season 7), 2.4x. Whale lapse rose 66% (0.81% → 1.34%).** Crash rate on low-tier devices went from 4.5% to 26.5% per session during v7.2 (`evidence/sql_checks.md`).

Today, LiveOps' win-back program is a rule: *"payers who haven't logged in for 3+ days get a discount, highest spenders first."* It has three problems:
1. **It's late.** By day 3 most lapsers are gone. Win-back offers convert about 2.5x better while the player is still playing (assumption table: 25% vs. 10%).
2. **It treats the symptom.** A whale who was double-charged needs their items restored, not a 20%-off bundle. A player crashing on a Galaxy A12 needs the hotfix and an apology gift.
3. **It's blind to what players say.** 27,395 support tickets and in-game feedback messages ("charged twice for gem pack… not spending another cent") never reach the LiveOps team. Players with a payment ticket lapse at **5.3%**, and those stating quit intent at **3.8%**, vs. **1.95%** overall.

## 2. How does the Databricks solution address it?

PlayerPulse is one integrated flow, orchestrated by a single Lakeflow Job:

1. **Lakeflow** ingests raw game-server telemetry, store receipts, tickets, the LiveOps calendar and accounts with Auto Loader. Bronze, silver and gold layers carry 13 data-quality expectations and produce title KPIs (DAU, ARPDAU, D1/D7/D30 retention, weekly churn by payer tier).
2. **Unity Catalog** governs the data: player PII (email, device ad-id, birth year) is masked, minor and consent status are tagged, and grants are least-privilege. Lineage is automatic and the model sits in the UC registry. A **responsible-monetization policy** (no paid offers to minors or players without consent) is enforced in the data and verified on every run.
3. **GenAI (Claude Haiku 5.5 via `ai_query`)** classifies every ticket: root cause, sentiment, explicit quit intent (handling negation), and severity.
4. **ML** predicts **P(no play in the next 14 days)** for every active player, from point-in-time weekly features plus the GenAI ticket signals. It's validated out-of-time and backtested against the current rule at equal capacity.
5. **Next-best-action** ranks players by **expected revenue at risk** and assigns a fix-first action (support escalation, tech fix + apology gift, energy refill, guild invite, personalized offer *only if eligible*). **Claude Sonnet 5.5** drafts a policy-constrained in-game message, and an automatic guardrail blocks any non-compliant draft.
6. **Lakebase** serves the retention queue to the app and the game backend: a synced table from Delta to Postgres, giving millisecond lookups at login. The app writes approved **interventions** to Postgres (OLTP), and these flow back to Delta as the treatment log.
7. **Genie** answers LiveOps, product and exec questions in plain English over the certified tables ("How did whale churn change from Season 6 to 7?", "Crash rate by device tier before and after v7.2?"). It's embedded in the app.
8. **The Databricks App** is the LiveOps console: command center, priority queue, player detail with the AI draft, approve-to-Lakebase, interventions board, and Genie chat.

## 3. Business outcomes and impact

Backtest: 10 out-of-time weeks (Aug–Oct 2026), the same 500 interventions per week as today's program. Assumptions are in `gold_liveops_assumptions` and can be changed there.

| KPI | Status quo (quiet 3+ days rule) | PlayerPulse |
|---|---|---|
| At-risk payer revenue reached (10 wks, 90-day spend of payers who lapsed) | $50.6K (68%) | **$64.6K (87%)** |
| Expected revenue retained, net of offer cost (10 wks) | $9.2K | **$16.3K (+78%)** |
| Incremental net revenue, annualized (this 26.6K-DAU title) | — | **+$37.3K** |
| **Incremental net revenue per 1M DAU, annualized** (linear scale-up) | — | **+$1.40M** |
| Players retained per year | 300 | 256 (see trade-off below) |
| Minors / non-consenting players given paid offers | not enforced | **0 (asserted every run)** |

Further outcomes for LiveOps and product, without a dollar figure attached:
- **Root-cause visibility:** Genie and the ticket insights showed the v7.2 low-tier Android crash spike within days.
- **Time to act:** from a monthly churn report to a daily queue, served in ms to the game backend.
- **Model quality:** out-of-time AUC **0.886** (PR-AUC 0.304, base rate 1.95%), vs. **0.835** for "days since last login" alone.
- **Serving:** 43,874 queue rows synced to Lakebase, matching Delta row for row; **3.1 ms** average login lookup.
- **GenAI:** 27,395 tickets classified with **0 parse errors**. 200 win-back drafts generated, with **0 guardrail violations**.

## Decisions and trade-offs

| Decision | Why | Trade-off / alternative considered |
|---|---|---|
| **Optimize *revenue at risk* (P × 90-day spend), not churn probability alone** | LiveOps capacity is the constraint. A 40%-risk whale matters more than a 90%-risk day-2 tourist. | Measured: it keeps **more revenue but fewer players** (256 vs. 300/yr). Mitigated by near-free actions (guild invite, event push) for non-payers outside the 500 slots. The objective is a config choice. |
| **14-day lapse label on players active in the last 7 days** | Matches how LiveOps acts (weekly), and the label is clean and quick to observe. | It misses players who go quiet and come back. A survival model or uplift model is the next step, once the treatment log has enough data. |
| **Gradient-boosted trees (sklearn HGB), not deep learning** | Tabular data, fast to retrain daily on serverless, and reason codes stay explainable for LiveOps. | Slightly less accurate than heavy feature learning; easy to swap behind the UC model alias. |
| **Fix-first next-best-action rules on top of the model** | Discounts don't fix crashes or billing bugs; the rules are transparent and auditable. | The rules are hand-written. Learning the action (contextual bandit) needs the intervention log this build starts collecting. |
| **GenAI for ticket classification, rather than keywords** | Handles slang and negation ("not quitting lol"), multi-issue tickets, and severity. | Adds token cost, kept small by classifying each ticket once, incrementally (47 s on rerun). Honest result: **no fleet-wide AUC lift** (0.886 with vs. 0.887 without); +0.002 among ticket filers. The value is root cause → the right action ("unresolved payment issue" drives most top-value queue rows), and insight via Genie. |
| **Claude via `ai_query` with JSON-in-prompt parsing** | Newer Claude models reject `ai_query`'s forced-tool structured output. Prompting for strict JSON and parsing with `from_json` works, and unparseable rows are logged and retried. | Less strict than schema-enforced decoding. A different endpoint (e.g. GPT/Gemini on Databricks) supports `responseFormat` if strict enforcement is required. |
| **Human approval of AI drafts, plus an automatic guardrail** | Player-facing text carries brand and regulatory risk (minors, dark patterns). | One click per intervention. Auto-send is possible for low-risk actions later. |
| **Lakebase synced table, not querying the SQL warehouse from the app or game** | Login-time lookups need ms latency and high concurrency. The app also needs OLTP writes. | A second copy of the data. Kept fresh incrementally (CDF, triggered by the daily job). |
| **Masks in the pipeline definition, not a separate masked view** | PII is never readable in clear text anywhere downstream, including by Genie. | Support staff need group membership to see PII. That's intended. |
| **Schemas in the shared `aws_demo_catalog`** | No catalog-create rights in the demo workspace. | In production: a dedicated `playerpulse` catalog per environment. |

## How AI was used to build this

The build was pair-programmed with **Claude Code** (Claude Opus 5.5) driving the Databricks CLI, Asset Bundles and REST APIs from a terminal:
- **Simulator design and calibration loop:** Claude wrote the behavioral simulator, then trained a local prototype model after each change to check the signal was learnable *before* building on it. That loop caught several problems:
  - a leak-onset bug in an earlier draft;
  - ticket signals that duplicated telemetry, which led to the "grievance" mechanic;
  - negative unit economics from poor assumptions;
  - an unrealistic 53% payer rate, recalibrated to a long-tailed whale curve.
- **Self-checking evidence:** `scripts/export_run_evidence.py` turns job runs into the readable Markdown in `evidence/`, and `sql_evidence.py` / `genie_evidence.py` capture warehouse and Genie output.
- **The deck is generated from data:** `deck/build_deck.py` builds the PPTX/PDF from `deck/deck_numbers.json`, whose numbers are copied from the backtest tables. The slides can't drift from the evidence.
- **Debugging in production:** every failure was diagnosed from real run output, verified on a tiny sample, then rerun in full. Examples:
  - newer Claude endpoints reject `ai_query` `responseFormat`, so the build switched to JSON-in-prompt plus `from_json`;
  - Claude sometimes returned `"severity": "low"`, fixed by parsing as string and using `try_cast`;
  - a `Decimal × float` bug;
  - CLI `--params` breaking on quotes, fixed with the `scripts/pg_admin.py` JSON path;
  - Genie honestly said it couldn't answer "crash rate by device tier", which exposed a missing dimension in `gold_daily_kpis`, now added.
- **Prompts** live in the notebooks. They encode domain rules (negation handling; responsible-monetization constraints for messages) and fixed output formats, so they can be parsed and tested.

## Demo script (tell, show, tell) and objection handling

**Tell (30 s):** "Season 7 doubled the lapse rate. Your win-back rule reaches payers on day 3 with a discount, when most are already gone and many needed a fix, not a coupon. PlayerPulse finds them 14 days ahead, picks the right fix, and lets your team act in one click."
**Show (4 min):**
1. App command center: revenue at risk and root causes.
2. Open a whale with a payment ticket: AI draft, policy banner, approve. The intervention lands in Lakebase.
3. Ask Genie: "crash rate by device tier before and after v7.2".
4. Briefly show the pipeline graph, UC lineage and masks, and the UC model registry.

**Tell (30 s):** the backtest table: same budget, 78% more revenue retained, about $1.4M per 1M DAU per year, and zero offers to minors.

| Objection | Answer |
|---|---|
| *Business:* "We already have a churn dashboard." | A dashboard tells you who left. This is a ranked, daily action queue with the root cause and a ready message, measured against your current rule on your own history. |
| *Business:* "Discounts train players to wait for deals." | Fix-first actions come before offers, and paid offers are capped by eligibility. The backtest charges the cost of every intervention. |
| *Business:* "How do we know it works on our real players?" | The backtest is out-of-time. The next step is a 4-week holdout A/B test (10% control), where the interventions table is the treatment log. |
| *Technical:* "The data is synthetic, so the AUC is meaningless." | Agreed for absolute numbers. The pipeline, features and backtest method are what carry over. The simulator is published, so the evaluation can be audited. |
| *Technical:* "Why Lakebase rather than a feature store or cache?" | The app needs OLTP writes (interventions), and the game backend needs PK lookups on curated rows. The synced table keeps one governed source of truth in UC. |
| *Technical:* "LLM cost and latency at our ticket volume?" | Each ticket is classified once, incrementally, with the cheapest capable model; messages are generated only for the top 200. The ticket model is a single config variable. |
| *Technical:* "PII and minors in Genie / the app?" | Masks are defined in the pipeline, so Genie and the app only ever see masked values. The minors policy is asserted on every run. |
